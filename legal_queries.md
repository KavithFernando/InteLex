# Example queries for learning how law is applied

**InteLex** is built for **law students**, **junior legal professionals**, and **legal researchers** who want to go beyond memorising articles and see **how courts have interpreted and applied** fundamental rights in real cases.

The questions below are phrased the way learners ask when preparing for tutorials, drafting research notes, or understanding judgment style. Each ties to **Sri Lankan Chapter III**–style issues in the corpus (interpretation frames). They are **illustrations**, not legal advice.

---

## How to use these queries

- Ask them in chat to trigger **case retrieval** and compare **how judges linked facts → constitutional text → outcome**.
- Use results to **read the matched frame’s legal issue, reasoning, and holding** alongside the cited article.
- Strong answers show **which provision** the court treated as central—not only generic “fundamental rights” language.
- For **Article 14**, try naming the **specific freedom** (e.g. assembly vs occupation) so results track **14(1)(b)** vs **14(1)(g)** rather than vague “rights” language.

---

## 1. From textbook rule to application (Article 12)

**Learning goal:** See how “equality before the law” is applied when the alleged wrongdoer is **not clearly the State** in a narrow sense.

**Query**

> I’m revising Article 12(1). How have the courts decided whether discrimination by a **public corporation** in employment counts as an infringement—especially when the body says its decision was **commercial**, not government policy?

**What useful retrieval looks like**

- Frames where **Article 12(1)** is central and the judgment discusses **state action**, **statutory corporations**, or **discretion** in employment.
- You can compare **how the court connected the facts** to the equality guarantee versus dismissing the claim for lack of jurisdiction.

---

## 2. Competing rights in a concrete setting (Article 10)

**Learning goal:** Understand **balancing**—religious freedom, public order, and control of sacred space.

**Query**

> For my essay on Article 10: how have judges treated **protests or assemblies** in **religious precincts** when authorities or police intervene? I need examples where the court explains **whose rights prevail** and on what basis.

**What useful retrieval looks like**

- Frames on **Article 10** involving **temples/shrines**, **permission**, and **police action**, with clear reasoning on **calm religious use** vs **political assembly**.
- Good for comparing **principled tests** (e.g. permission, disturbance) rather than only abstract statements of religious freedom.

---

## 3. Reading the Constitution with procedure in mind (Article 13)

**Learning goal:** Anchor **criminal procedure FRs** to **what the court required in practice** on arrest and information.

**Query**

> I’m trying to learn Article 13(1) properly. What do reported cases say about **informing a person of the reason for arrest**, and how strict is that requirement in the case law we have?

**What useful retrieval looks like**

- Frames tied to **Article 13** subclauses on **arrest** and **information of reasons**, with **application_to_facts**-style reasoning you can quote in a problem question.

---

## 4. Researching a newer textual right (Article 14A)

**Learning goal:** Map **access to information** against **lawful restrictions** “in a democratic society.”

**Query**

> For my research note on **RTI-style** rights: how does our case law treat **access to information held by public authorities**, and what kinds of **restrictions** does the court treat as acceptable?

**What useful retrieval looks like**

- Frames on **Article 14A** (if present in your corpus) describing **scope of “information”**, **who counts as a public authority**, and **legitimate restrictions**.
- If few cases appear, that itself tells you **where the literature or corpus is thin**—a valid research finding.

---

## 5. The “existing law” puzzle (Article 16)

**Learning goal:** Grasp **Article 16** as a **savings clause** and see how courts **phrase the relationship** between old statutes and Chapter III.

**Query**

> I don’t fully understand Article 16 in practice. Can you point me to judgments that explain whether **pre-Constitution laws** can still apply **even if they look inconsistent** with fundamental rights—and how the court justifies that?

**What useful retrieval looks like**

- Frames where **Article 16(1)** or **16(2)** is discussed in **holding or reasoning**, not only in passing.
- Helps separate **exam answers** (“what the article says”) from **applied law** (“how courts use the savings clause”).

---

## 6. The route to the Supreme Court (Article 17)

**Learning goal:** Connect **substantive rights** to **who may apply** and **when** executive or administrative action is challengeable.

**Query**

> As a junior researcher I need clarity on **Article 17**: who may **apply to the Supreme Court** for infringement of fundamental rights, and what do cases say **“infringement or imminent infringement”** requires in practice?

**What useful retrieval looks like**

- Frames where **Article 17** (and related **remedy** language) shapes the **issue** or **outcome**—standing, timeliness, or scope of **executive/administrative action**.
- Useful for **moot problems** and for reading **application (FR) judgments** systematically.

---

## Note on retrieval quality

If many queries still return **Article 14(1)** broadly, try **naming the article or fact pattern** (as above), or follow up with **“under Article 12 only”** / **“Article 10 and religious places”** to steer semantic search. Improving embeddings and **diversifying by article** in the backend also helps (see project docs).

---

## Disclaimer

These examples support **learning and research habits**. They do not replace **primary sources**, **supervision**, or **professional advice**. Always verify citations and holdings against the **full judgment** and current law.
